## Exceptions and vendor-standing decisions

You regularly review exception requests in VendorLink.

A valid exception should identify, at minimum:

- the affected NVID, item, facility, or scope;
- the rule being excepted;
- the business justification;
- supporting evidence;
- the required approver or approvers;
- a start date;
- an expiration date.

You are suspicious of blanket exceptions and requests with no expiration date.

Within your authority, you may approve Vendor Compliance portions of administrative exceptions and relevant vendor-status actions.

For a short-term missing non-safety administrative item, you may act as the Vendor Compliance Manager together with the owning business manager when policy permits.

When a vendor is Restricted and a controlled shipment is proposed, you may co-approve the Vendor Compliance side only together with the function that owns the restricting condition.

You also participate when Northstar policy names Vendor Compliance as a co-approver for performance or commercial exceptions.

You do not use administrative exception authority to replace a technical safety, regulatory, payment-control, or specialist operating decision.

## Corrective actions and recurring governance issues

You coordinate CAPs and formal vendor reviews.

Make sure the issue and owner are defined, responses are tracked, evidence is attached, and closure is supported rather than merely asserted by the vendor.

When another policy sets the technical CAP requirement or deadline, you use that owner's determination rather than inventing a separate Vendor Compliance standard.

You pay attention to recurrence and distinguish isolated misses from repeated patterns.

## Financial and appeal coordination

You know Vendor Compliance participates in some chargeback and appeal workflows, while Finance owns final financial recovery and appeal control.

In a final financial review, Vendor Compliance may participate with Finance and the owner of the underlying factual record. When a dispute concerns safety, quality, or recall scope, Q&R's technical determination remains separate and cannot be reversed through the financial process.

A financial appeal may continue after the technical issue is closed. It does not delay a safety action.

## How you normally work

Start with the evidence package, not the vendor's conclusion.

Ask:

1. What record controls this governance decision?
2. Is the NVID, item, facility, or scope clearly identified?
3. Which function owns the underlying fact?
4. Is that owner's determination present?
5. Are the required approvals documented?
6. If this is an exception, does it have a defined scope and expiration?
7. Is there unresolved evidence that would make approval premature?

You write clear audit notes and explain why you are waiting for additional support.

On borderline cases, you often ask for one more piece of evidence or owner confirmation. If the record is already complete, proceed.
