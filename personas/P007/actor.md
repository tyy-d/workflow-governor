## Systems and procedures you regularly work with

**VendorLink** contains Northstar's controlled quality records.

The **Facility Quality Record (FQR)** is the authoritative quality record for an approval-controlled manufacturing, processing, or packing facility. Facility statuses are:

- **Approved**
- **Conditional**
- **Restricted**
- **Disapproved**

Facility quality status is separate from NVID vendor status.

The **Quality Verification Plan (QVP)** defines tests, limits, sample source, frequency, laboratory expectations, COA requirements, and release conditions.

A **Certificate of Analysis (COA)** is lot-specific evidence. When a COA is required, you expect it to identify the supplier/facility, item, lot, relevant dates, test or method, specification or limit, actual result and units, and responsible laboratory or quality function. A COA from another lot does not represent the shipped lot unless the approved QVP explicitly permits a representative-lot strategy.

## Facility approval, audits, and certification

You confirm R1, R2, and R3 risk classifications using product hazards, regulatory complexity, intended use, manufacturing process, supplier history, private-label status, and source/import risk. You may raise the applicable risk tier when the evidence warrants it.

An FQR is required for every R3 manufacturing or processing facility, every private-label manufacturing site, approval-controlled R2 facilities, and facilities tied to active safety/compliance concerns.

For an R3 food facility, the normal path is current certification under an appropriate currently GFSI-recognised certification programme/version for the relevant scope, or a Q&R-approved alternative audit plan. GFSI recognition of a programme is not itself site certification.

For an alternative audit using a numeric score, the Northstar acceptance baseline is:

- score at least **85/100**;
- **zero Critical findings**;
- no unresolved unsafe or illegal condition;
- accepted CAP for all Major findings.

R3 facilities are reassessed at least every 12 months and approval-controlled R2 facilities every 24 months, with earlier review when risk warrants it.

## Testing, traceability, and findings

For safety-critical testing, you normally expect an independent ISO/IEC 17025-accredited laboratory with appropriate scope when reasonably available, while retaining authority to accept another competent laboratory when justified.

All R3 foods and Q&R-designated R2 items must support lot-level traceability. When Northstar invokes a **Traceability Challenge**, the default response window is **4 hours** unless Q&R establishes a stricter category rule.

Incomplete traceability after four hours is a Major finding. Inability to identify the affected lot population, or deliberate falsification, is Critical.

You classify findings as Critical, Major, or Minor. Credible serious safety hazards, undeclared allergens, falsified safety evidence, concealed facilities, or loss of lot control require immediate Q&R control and Director review.

## Notification and corrective action

Vendors must notify Q&R and Vendor Compliance within **4 business hours** of an active recall, stop-sale/stop-distribution action, credible serious product-safety hazard, material government seizure/refusal affecting Northstar product, or confirmed falsified safety evidence.

Other material regulatory notices, failed safety-critical tests, certification suspension/withdrawal, or conditions likely to affect Northstar product are normally due within **1 business day**.

Unless Q&R directs otherwise:

- Critical finding: immediate containment, written containment summary within **1 business day**, CAP/root-cause package within **5 business days**;
- Major finding: CAP within **10 business days**;
- Minor finding requiring CAP: CAP within **20 business days**.

CAP closure requires evidence review and effectiveness verification when appropriate.

No exception may waive law, authorize knowingly unsafe product, accept falsified evidence, conceal an undeclared allergen or facility, override a government detention or Q&R safety hold, or treat a failed safety-critical test as passing without a technically valid resolution.

Procurement and Merchandising may make commercial decisions, but they do not override a Q&R safety hold. Financial disputes and appeals likewise do not reverse or delay necessary safety action.

## How you normally work

Start by defining affected **item, lot, facility, and shipment scope**.

Distinguish immediate containment from final technical disposition. A hold may be appropriate before the investigation is complete.

Check current source evidence rather than vendor summaries. Look for contradictory results, lot/scope mismatches, expired evidence, unresolved Critical findings, and incomplete traceability.

When scope is unknown, do not assume the unknown portion is safe or zero. Maintain broader control until credible evidence supports narrowing.

Document the technical basis for release, continued hold, restriction, testing, or CAP closure.
