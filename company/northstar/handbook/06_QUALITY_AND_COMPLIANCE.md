# Quality and Compliance

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy defines Northstar Regional Markets requirements for product quality, regulatory evidence, food-safety verification, facility approval, testing, traceability readiness, and corrective action.

It applies to all vendors and products. Controls increase from R1 Standard to R2 Controlled to R3 Enhanced Food/Safety. Private-label food remains R3.

Quality & Regulatory (Q&R) owns technical approval, food/product-safety decisions, regulatory evidence review, facility approval, testing requirements, and safety holds. Vendor Compliance coordinates records, corrective-action administration, and vendor-status consequences. Procurement and Merchandising own commercial decisions but may not override a Q&R safety hold.

Applicable law remains controlling; Northstar policy does not create or waive legal requirements.

## 2. Definitions

**Facility Quality Record (FQR)** — VendorLink record for an approved or proposed manufacturing/processing/packing facility. It identifies facility legal name and address, products/processes, risk tier, regulatory status, certifications/audits, open findings, and Northstar facility status.

**Facility status** — `Approved`, `Conditional`, `Restricted`, or `Disapproved`. Facility status is separate from the NVID vendor status defined in `00_COMPANY_AND_VENDOR_OVERVIEW.md`.

**Quality Verification Plan (QVP)** — item or product-family record specifying required tests, specifications, sample source, frequency, laboratory expectations, COA requirements, and release conditions.

**Certificate of Analysis (COA)** — lot-specific test result record issued by the manufacturer or laboratory and linked to the lot represented.

**Critical Quality Event (CQE)** — evidence of an actual or credible condition that could create serious safety, regulatory, identity, or traceability risk, including an undeclared allergen, pathogen concern, falsified test/certificate, prohibited substance, material regulatory action affecting Northstar product, or loss of control over affected lots.

**Corrective Action Plan (CAP)** — documented containment, root-cause analysis, corrective/preventive actions, owners, due dates, evidence, and effectiveness verification.

## 3. Vendor responsibilities

Vendors must:

1. manufacture, pack, store, and release Northstar products only from disclosed and approved facilities when facility approval is required;
2. maintain all legally applicable registrations, licenses, permits, food-safety plans, HACCP plans, preventive-control programs, sanitation controls, allergen controls, food-defense measures, and other regulatory programs;
3. operate under documented GMP or equivalent manufacturing-quality controls appropriate to the product;
4. maintain specifications and records sufficient to show each shipped lot conforms to the approved product and packaging requirements;
5. provide current certificates, audit reports, regulatory records, test reports, COAs, and investigation records requested by Q&R;
6. use qualified laboratories and competent auditors appropriate to the test or audit being performed;
7. preserve sample and lot identity so results can be linked to the production represented;
8. notify Q&R and Vendor Compliance of material safety/compliance events within the time limits below; and
9. cooperate with Northstar investigations, traceability exercises, CAPs, and reasonable facility audits.

A broker or distributor may transmit records but may not conceal the manufacturer, source facility, lot, or responsible party.

## 4. Northstar quality and facility-approval process

### 4.1 Initial risk review

Q&R confirms R1/R2/R3 using product hazards, regulatory complexity, intended use, manufacturing process, supplier history, private-label status, and source/import risk.

An FQR is required for:
- every R3 manufacturing or processing facility;
- every private-label manufacturing site;
- any R2 facility Q&R designates as approval-controlled; and
- any facility tied to an active Northstar safety/compliance concern.

### 4.2 Facility approval

| Tier / condition | Default review |
|---|---|
| R1 | document review or event-driven review; FQR only if designated |
| R2 approval-controlled | facility questionnaire, regulatory evidence, relevant quality records, and risk-based audit/testing |
| R3 | FQR, applicable regulatory evidence, food-safety documentation, current acceptable certification/audit evidence, and Q&R approval |
| New private-label site | requirements above plus private-label gates in `03_PRIVATE_LABEL_PRODUCTS.md` |

For an R3 food manufacturing facility, the default evidence is certification against a **currently GFSI-recognised certification programme and version for the facility's relevant scope**, issued by an appropriate certification body, or a written Northstar-approved alternative audit plan.

A certificate is acceptable only when:
- facility legal name/address match or the relationship is credibly documented;
- certified scope covers the activity producing Northstar product;
- certificate is current;
- programme/version is recognised for the applicable GFSI scope when GFSI recognition is claimed; and
- there is no known suspension, withdrawal, critical finding, regulatory condition, or contradictory evidence that makes reliance unreasonable.

GFSI itself does not certify the facility.

### 4.3 Alternative audit plan

When Q&R approves an alternative to the normal R3 certification path, the facility must undergo an independent or Northstar-directed food-safety audit at least every 12 months unless Q&R specifies a shorter interval.

Northstar's fictional alternative-audit acceptance threshold is:
- score **>=85/100** where a numeric score is used;
- **zero Critical findings**;
- no unresolved condition indicating product may be unsafe or illegally produced; and
- accepted CAP for all Major findings.

### 4.4 Ongoing review

Q&R performs:
- annual certification/status review for R3 facilities;
- formal FQR reassessment at least every 12 months for R3 and every 24 months for approval-controlled R2 facilities;
- event-driven reassessment after serious complaints, regulatory action, failed tests, recalls, material process changes, audit deterioration, or repeated CAP failure.

Q&R may require an announced or unannounced audit, expanded testing, or shortened review interval based on risk.

## 5. Required documents and data

Depending on product and risk, the FQR/QVP may require:

| Evidence | Typical applicability |
|---|---|
| facility registration/license/permit evidence | where legally applicable |
| food-safety plan or HACCP/risk analysis | applicable food facilities |
| GFSI-program certificate and scope | default R3 food path |
| full audit report / CAP | when Q&R requires review beyond certificate |
| allergen-control program | foods with allergen exposure |
| sanitation/environmental-monitoring program | risk-appropriate food operations |
| recall and traceability procedure | R2/R3 food and designated non-food |
| food-defense plan/attestation | where legally required or Q&R-designated |
| product specification / APS | controlled products; APS for private label |
| QVP and laboratory test reports | risk/designated products |
| lot-specific COA | when QVP or PO requires it |
| shelf-life validation evidence | when required under `02_PRODUCT_FRESHNESS.md` |
| food-contact / chemical / safety evidence | applicable packaging/general merchandise |
| regulatory correspondence affecting Northstar items | when material |

Q&R may request source records when summaries or certificates are insufficient.

## 6. Testing, COAs, and verification standards

### 6.1 Quality Verification Plan

Q&R establishes a QVP when routine or event-triggered testing is needed. The QVP identifies the characteristic tested, specification/limit, method or standard, sample type, frequency, laboratory, and release rule.

Q&R considers testing:
- before initial private-label approval;
- after a Material Change that could affect safety, legality, composition, performance, or claims;
- when a regulatory standard or approved specification requires testing;
- after a relevant failed result or credible complaint trend; and
- periodically for categories Q&R identifies as higher risk.

For private-label products, testing required by the QVP is part of the technical release process in file 03. ### 6.2 Laboratory expectations

For regulatory, chemical, microbiological, or safety-critical testing, Q&R normally requires an independent ISO/IEC 17025-accredited laboratory for an appropriate scope when reasonably available. Q&R may accept another competent laboratory when justified.

Known contradictory results from the same investigation or lot may not be withheld.

### 6.3 COA controls

When a QVP or PO requires a COA, it must contain enough information to identify:
- supplier and manufacturing facility;
- item/SKU;
- production lot/batch;
- manufacture or test date;
- test/method;
- specification or acceptance limit;
- actual result and units;
- laboratory or responsible quality function; and
- release/approval identity where applicable.

A COA for a different lot is not evidence for the shipped lot unless the approved QVP explicitly uses a validated representative-lot strategy.

If Northstar requires COA-before-release, product may not ship until the required passing COA is linked in VendorLink or the approved electronic record.

## 7. Traceability and food-safety readiness

All R3 foods and Q&R-designated R2 items must support lot-level traceability from the Northstar shipment back to the manufacturing lot and relevant source records.

Northstar may conduct a **Traceability Challenge**. Unless Q&R sets a stricter category rule, the vendor must provide within **4 hours**:
- affected Northstar item and lot identifiers;
- quantity produced and disposition;
- Northstar POs/shipments containing the lot;
- immediate upstream source information relevant to the implicated material; and
- other customers/consignees when needed for a safety investigation and legally permissible.

Incomplete traceability after four hours is a Major finding; inability to identify the affected lot population or deliberate falsification is a Critical finding.

For foods subject to FDA's Food Traceability Rule, vendors must prepare for applicable KDE/CTE obligations. As of September 2026, FDA states it will not enforce the rule before **July 20, 2028**. Northstar's four-hour challenge is separate company policy.

When Northstar is the FSVP importer, Q&R maintains or oversees the applicable FSVP evaluation and verification process. Verification may include qualified onsite audits, sampling/testing, food-safety record review, or other risk-appropriate activities. Applicable FSVP reevaluation occurs at least every three years or sooner when required by new hazard or supplier-performance information.

## 8. Deviations, corrective action, and escalation

### 8.1 Finding severity

| Severity | Examples | Default action |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | credible serious safety hazard; undeclared allergen; falsified safety evidence; prohibited/illegal condition; loss of lot control; concealed facility; failed critical process control | immediate Q&R Hold; Director Q&R review |
| Major | significant system failure without confirmed serious hazard; failed required test; repeated traceability gap; expired required certification; multiple audit failures | hold/restriction as risk warrants; CAP |
| Minor | isolated documentation or housekeeping weakness with no material safety/legality impact | correction or CAP as assigned |

Q&R may elevate severity based on recurrence, regulatory significance, or inability to bound affected product.

### 8.2 Notification

A vendor must notify Q&R and Vendor Compliance:
- within **4 business hours** after learning of an active recall, stop-sale/stop-distribution action, credible serious product-safety hazard, government seizure/refusal materially affecting Northstar product, or confirmed falsification of safety evidence; and
- within **1 business day** for other material regulatory notices, failed safety-critical tests, certification suspension/withdrawal, or conditions reasonably likely to affect Northstar product.

Notification must identify known items/lots, facilities, shipments, containment, and material unknowns.

### 8.3 CAP deadlines

Unless Q&R states otherwise:
- Critical finding: immediate containment; written containment summary within **1 business day**; CAP/root-cause package within **5 business days**.
- Major finding: CAP within **10 business days**.
- Minor finding requiring CAP: CAP within **20 business days**.

Critical/Major corrective actions should normally be completed within **30 calendar days** and Minor actions within **60 calendar days**. If completion is not feasible, the vendor must request an extension before the due date with interim controls, evidence, owner, and revised date.

CAP closure requires evidence review and, when appropriate, effectiveness verification.

### 8.4 Status consequences

Q&R may place affected items/lots on hold, make a facility Conditional or Restricted, require enhanced testing, suspend technical release, or designate the facility Disapproved. Vendor Compliance may separately change the NVID vendor status under file 00.

A Disapproved facility may not produce new Northstar product.

## 9. Exceptions and non-waivable controls

A time-limited certification-continuity exception may be approved only by the **Director, Quality & Regulatory**. It must be approved before certificate expiry, identify affected facility/items, include evidence of scheduled recertification or an alternative audit, confirm no open Critical findings, and expire no later than **45 calendar days** after the prior certificate's expiration unless a documented legal/regulatory circumstance requires a different treatment.

Testing or documentation exceptions require Q&R approval appropriate to the risk; commercial managers cannot waive technical requirements merely to preserve supply.

No exception may:
- waive applicable law;
- authorize knowingly unsafe or adulterated product;
- accept falsified audit/test/COA data;
- conceal an undeclared allergen or manufacturing site;
- override a government detention or Northstar Q&R safety hold; or
- treat a failed safety-critical test as passing without technically valid resolution.

## 10. Records and evidence

VendorLink preserves FQRs, QVPs, certifications, audit evidence, test reports, COAs, findings, CAPs, exceptions, regulatory notices, and traceability-challenge records.

Northstar retains quality/compliance records for **7 years after the later of the last Northstar receipt from the affected facility or retirement of the affected item**, unless law, investigation, contract, or another Northstar policy requires longer retention.

## 11. Examples / scenarios

**Scenario A — valid certificate, wrong scope.** A frozen-food factory submits a current certificate from a GFSI-recognised programme, but the certified scope covers warehousing only. Q&R cannot treat it as satisfying the manufacturing-facility requirement; the FQR remains Conditional until suitable evidence is approved.

**Scenario B — audit score 91 with Critical finding.** An alternative audit scores 91/100 but identifies an uncontrolled allergen-cleaning failure classified Critical. The facility does not pass Northstar's alternative-audit rule because zero Critical findings are required.

**Scenario C — failed private-label test.** A private-label household product fails a required chemical limit during first-production testing. Q&R places the lot on hold and withholds Shipping Authorized. Procurement cannot release it because the PO is commercially urgent.

**Scenario D — traceability delay.** An R3 supplier identifies the relevant production lot in two hours but cannot link it to Northstar shipments until hour six. The Traceability Challenge is a Major finding because the four-hour company threshold was missed, even if no unsafe product is ultimately found.
